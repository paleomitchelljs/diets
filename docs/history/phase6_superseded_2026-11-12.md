### Phase 6. Model rebuild and fossil comparison

Four steps, each its own session. All read only from `db/`, all write under `code/db_analysis/` and `results/`, and none modify the old scripts. The numbered problems (M1–M12) and improvements are in "Review of the statistical model" above.

#### 6a. Descriptive baseline and design audit

> **Prompt 6a.** Read `docs/ASSESSMENT_AND_PLAN_2026-11.md`, especially "Review of the statistical model", then `db/SCHEMA.md`. Using only `db/` (tetrapod predators, vertebrate prey unless stated), produce a descriptive baseline with no fitted hierarchical model. Deliver: a table of row, species and study counts for every clade by observation type by tier cell; for each cell with data, ln prey mass against ln predator mass with conditional 10th, 50th and 90th percentile curves from quantile regression or binned quantiles; the number of distinct predator-mass values per species and per clade, to show how much within-species size variation exists; the share of prey items that are vertebrates as a function of predator mass by clade (this needs the invertebrate rows); and the fossil points overlaid by evidence class with their mass intervals. Take clade from `db/taxa.csv`; do not assign clades by matching genus names. Write `results/6a_descriptive.md` stating, for each planned fossil comparison (ingested prey vs stomach, pellet vs pellet and scat, combat vs follow), which extant clades have enough data to serve as a reference and which do not, using a stated minimum of species and studies. Do not fit a Bayesian model in this step.

#### 6b. Reference model

> **Prompt 6b.** Read `docs/ASSESSMENT_AND_PLAN_2026-11.md` ("Review of the statistical model", problems M1–M12 and the improvements list), `results/6a_descriptive.md`, and the old `code/x20_gutSlopesStan.R` for context only. Build a new extant reference model in `code/db_analysis/` with brms or Stan. Requirements: (1) split ln predator mass into species mean and within-species deviation and estimate separate among-species and within-species slopes, the latter from individual pairs with a measured predator mass (`individual_pair` TRUE, tiers A–B); (2) observation-type offsets that vary by clade with partial pooling, and a table saying for each clade and observation type whether the offset is informed by that clade's data; (3) handle the vertebrate-only filter explicitly, by a two-part model (probability an item is a vertebrate, then size given vertebrate) or by fitting all animal prey with a prey-type term, and show how the vertebrate-prey lines differ from the filtered fit; (4) compare residual families (Gaussian, skew-normal, Student-t with a prior keeping degrees of freedom above 2, two-component mixture) by leave-one-study-out predictive performance and posterior predictive checks on the 5th and 95th percentiles; (5) no likelihood weights: represent mass uncertainty as measurement error with a log-scale standard deviation per `mass_method`, stating the source or rationale for each value; (6) no subsampling by default, with study and species effects; if computation forces caps, report results across at least 20 seeds and three cap sizes; (7) a phylogenetic species effect if `db/tree/` exists, otherwise a nested taxonomic effect and a note; (8) priors written down with a prior predictive check and a sensitivity run with every prior scale doubled. Fit cumulatively by tier (A, A–B, A–C, A–D, A–E) and save each fit. Also fit three weightings for the fossil comparison: per prey item, largest item per predator record, and biomass-weighted. Use one core per chain if chains hang at warm-up. Deliver `results/6b_reference_model.md` with estimates, diagnostics (R-hat, effective sample size, divergences), and a plain statement of which parameters the data do not identify.

#### 6c. Validation

> **Prompt 6c.** Read `docs/ASSESSMENT_AND_PLAN_2026-11.md` and `results/6b_reference_model.md`. Test whether the reference model and the placement procedure can be trusted before any fossil is placed. (1) Calibration: for each clade with stomach data, hold out whole studies in turn, refit, place the held-out extant records exactly as fossils would be placed, and test whether their predictive percentiles are uniform; repeat holding out whole species. Report coverage of 50 and 90 percent intervals by clade. (2) Recoverability: simulate data on the real design matrix (the actual clade by observation-type by tier cell counts) with known observation-type offsets, including offsets that differ among clades, refit, and report bias and interval coverage for those offsets; state which offsets are recoverable. (3) Cross-observation check: where one clade has two observation types, predict one from the other and report the error. (4) Sensitivity of the main estimates to tier, weighting, residual family, prior scale, and removal of each of the three largest studies per clade. Deliver `results/6c_validation.md` ending with a list of the fossil comparisons that are licensed by these tests and those that are not. Do not tune the model to pass; report failures.

#### 6d. Fossil placement

> **Prompt 6d.** Read `docs/ASSESSMENT_AND_PLAN_2026-11.md`, `results/6b_reference_model.md` and `results/6c_validation.md`. Place fossil records from `db/pairs.csv` against the extant reference, only for comparisons that 6c licensed. Rules: match observation type (ingested prey against stomach contents; fossil pellets against extant pellets and scat, kept as separate levels unless 6b showed them indistinguishable; combat associations against follow studies including failed attacks); draw predator and prey masses from their intervals in the database on every posterior draw; treat multiple prey in one predator jointly; use the within-species slope for individual specimens and say so; do not place a fossil whose predator mass lies outside the supported range of a reference clade, and list those cases. For each fossil report the predictive percentile with an interval against every eligible clade, and the log predictive density under each clade with relative support, so that "which living group does this resemble" is answered by model comparison. Repeat for each tier stop (A, A–B, A–C, A–D, A–E) and each weighting, and save tidy outputs for the site's tier slider. Deliver `results/6d_fossil_placement.md` stating what holds across all tiers and weightings, what changes, how the conclusions differ from the current README headline (theropods below same-size birds; resemblance to lizards), and why.
