# Phase 4b expansion report: widening the extant data in `db/`

Written 2026-11-12, updated the same day after two instructions from JM (assume SquamataBase is correct; incorporate the Pianka data), for Prompt 4b of `docs/ASSESSMENT_AND_PLAN_2026-11.md`. Numbers come from `db/` and `db/migration/*.csv`; the tables below are generated by `code/db_migrate/expansion_report.R` (copy in `db/migration/expansion_tables.md`).

## Result in brief

- **Part 3 (open datasets) is done.** SquamataBase v1.0.0, CarniDIET 1.0 and GATEWAy 1.0 were already on disk from Phase 0 (`data/structured/raw_downloads/`), so network access was only needed for two trait databases. They add 38,924 pairs (29,065 with both masses; 5,797 individual pairs, all from SquamataBase) and 1,452 predator taxa.
- **Pianka data are in** (script 33): 518 individual lizards and snakes that ate vertebrates, from `OZ_all.csv` (Australia, 461 animals) and `NA_all.csv` (North America, 57 animals; 5 of the 520 vertebrate-eating animals were dropped as ambiguous predators). Prey are `Vertebrata_indet` (Australia) and `Lacertilia` (North America) with the prey volume as a remains measurement, so these rows have no prey mass and are not usable for ratio fits. Not ingested: Kalahari `KL_all.csv` (prey are numeric codes 1 to 68 and the folder has no key), and the invertebrate prey categories (about 50,000 rows; not implemented, it needs a prey-category-to-taxon table).
- **SquamataBase records are taken as correct** (your instruction): the recorded prey mass is now `measured` (381 rows), no longer `unknown`. Those rows are tier A where the predator mass was also measured or regressed and tier C where it is a species mean. The 381 `unknown_method` warnings are gone (validator warnings 953 to 572).
- **Part 2 (prey masses) is done for what the databases support.** 52,267 pairs had a predator mass and no prey mass. 39,162 now have a prey mass. 13,105 do not, listed in `db/migration/prey_mass_remaining.csv`. Two trait databases were downloaded for this, the Amniote database (reptile masses) and AmphiBIO (amphibian masses).
- **Part 1 (PDFs on disk) is only partly done.** I extracted 3 files: the two skipped *Lanius excubitor* PDFs (81 pairs) and the carpet-python paper in `ai_found/` (29 pairs). Tryjanowski et al. 2003 yields nothing usable. Of the roughly 210 files in scope, 172 were not extracted. Most are the 112 raptor, owl and mammal papers that the June 2026 screening filed without extraction. The ledger below gives the reason for each file. I did not work through them.
- `db/` now holds 104,982 pairs (104,908 extant), 3,807 sources and 10,853 taxa. The validator passes with 0 errors. `db/tests/run_tests.R` passes. The 74 fossil pairs are unchanged.
- **Ready for fitting:** extant vertebrate-prey pairs with both masses number 76,162: 429 tier A, 626 B, 55 C, 59,805 D, 15,247 E. Restricted to individual pairs: 376 A, 575 B, 36 C, 1,386 D, 1,768 E. The tier D count includes 31,000+ CarniDIET, OS-Prey and GATEWAy rows that are population-level, so select on `individual_pair` and `observation_type` as decision 1 and 4 require. The one remaining blocker for a clean `run_all.R` is the fossil script failure below.
- The new scripts are idempotent: two consecutive runs of scripts 20 to 90 left `pairs.csv`, `sources.csv` and `taxa.csv` byte-identical.

## Problems you need to know about

1. **`code/db_migrate/10_fossil_step2.R` fails.** `run_all.R` stops there with `Error in sprintf("RUNG 1. Body length 120-130 cm ...": argument is missing` (a stray `%` in a format string, or a missing argument, in the Ceratopsia rung text). The file is untracked and was modified at 13:23 on 2026-11-12, so I did not touch it (fossil rows are off limits in this phase). The failure comes before scripts 20 to 90, so I ran those directly. The 74 fossil pairs are still in `db/` because no script deletes them. Fix the fossil script, then `run_all.R` runs end to end.
2. **`90_prey_mass_fill.R` edits rows owned by other scripts.** It is a post-processing step that fills empty prey-mass fields, tags each filled row in `notes` (`[Phase 4b prey fill; tier before: X]`) and undoes its own fills on rerun. Rerunning any one of scripts 01 to 32 alone removes the fills for its rows until 90 is run again. `run_all.R` runs it last.
3. **Order-level typical masses push many old rows to tier E.** 22,869 filled rows moved to `E_coarse_or_aggregate` (`assigned_approximate` forces tier E under SCHEMA.md). That includes 1 row that was tier A. Species counts in tier D therefore fall in some cells of the tables below, and tier E rises. The "before" columns undo this using the tag. Analyses that use tiers A to D will see fewer rows from Part 2 than the 39,162 headline suggests: only 15,648 + 585 rows kept their tier.

Duplicate rule, as in `MIGRATION_REPORT.md`: a block of records is a duplicate when db/ already holds, from another source, the same publication with the same predator species and the same evidence kind (gut or other). SquamataBase has no author field, so its duplicates come from a hand-checked map (`evidence/expansion/squamatabase_duplicates.csv`, 7 publications, matched by year, journal and title). CarniDIET and GATEWAy are matched on first-author surname, year, predator species and evidence kind. The db copy is kept. GATEWAy had no match. The limitation: a duplicate whose first author is spelled differently, or whose predator carries a different scientific name, is missed.

## Decisions and limits for JM

- **SquamataBase masses are taken as measured** (predator and, for single-prey rows, prey) without checking the original sources, on your instruction.
- **Genus-mean fallbacks.** When a species name is not found in a trait database (mostly synonymy: CoL 2016 against Amniote 2015, for example *Lacerta vivipara*), the genus mean is used and coded `species_average_database` with the basis saying "genus mean", as `04_raptordiets.R` did. 2,293 filled prey rows and many predator rows are of this kind. Filter on the basis text to remove them.
- **No invertebrate length–mass equations were used.** No prey row in `db/` has a source-given body length for an invertebrate, and no invertebrate equation is registered in `docs/methodology_index.csv`. Invertebrates and fishes get the median GATEWAy resource mass for the most specific taxon GATEWAy holds, with a min–max interval, as `assigned_approximate`.
- **Non-adult prey were not filled** (egg, juvenile, larva and so on: 776 rows), since an adult mean would misstate them.
- **Remaining 13,105 rows without a prey mass.** 7,845 are class-level prey (Aves 3,611, Mammalia 2,160, Reptilia 916, Amphibia 424, Invertebrata 425). The rest are taxa that match nothing at their own rank or above (Cricetidae and Arvicolinae are name mismatches with PanTHERIA's taxonomy and could be fixed with a synonym table; Scorpiones, Haplotaxida, Solifugae and Chilopoda are absent from GATEWAy).
- **SquamataBase master** (about 100 records added after v1.0.0) is on disk and not ingested.
- Pianka archive: only the vertebrate-prey animals are in. If you want the 50,000 invertebrate-prey rows too (they would get order-level GATEWAy masses, tier E), I would have to write a prey-category-to-taxon table first; say so and I will. For the Kalahari data, send the key to prey codes 1 to 68.
- `data/intake_manifest.csv` was not edited (old files stay unchanged), so its `queued` statuses are stale for the files in the ledger. Also, 166 of its 182 queued paths no longer exist because files were moved in June.
- Next-session candidates from the ledger: Lake Griffin alligators, King 1980 weasel prey table, Somaweera 2018 freshwater crocodile observations, Parker & Pianka 1976 and the collared-lizard papers, Knopff cougar kill records.
