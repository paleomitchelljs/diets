## Hu2005: Hu et al. 2005 Nature, *Repenomamus*

- Specimens: "Holotype. A partial skull with complete right upper dentition, associated right mandible with complete lower dentition, and articulated postcranium with pes and manus missing, IVPP ... V14155" (R. giganticus; no gut contents). Gut specimen = R. robustus IVPP V13605 (Fig. 3: "Associated partial skull and lower jaws not illustrated").
- Location: "a patch of small bones was revealed within the ribcage, on the ventral sides of the posterior left thoracic ribs and vertebrae, where the stomach is positioned in extant mammals" (p.151).
- ID: "Unduplicated dentitions, limb bones and phalanges in the patch confirm that these bones belong to a juvenile individual of Psittacosaurus" (p.151). "There are at least seven teeth on each jaw quadrant ... most are worn. This demonstrates that the Psittacosaurus skeleton is not from an embryo." 
- Condition: "The skull and most of the skeleton of the juvenile Psittacosaurus are broken, disarticulated and displaced ... A few long bones are preserved in articulation ... suggesting that the juvenile Psittacosaurus was dismembered and swallowed as chunks."
- Prey measurements: "Measurements of the juvenile Psittacosaurus (length in mm): humerus, 21; radius, 18; ulna, 19; tibia, 36; and fibula, 35." SI: "Tooth lengths: lower tooth 1 --2.1mm; lower tooth 2 – 2.1mm; lower tooth 3 – 2.2mm; upper tooth 6 – 2.4mm; upper tooth 7 –2.8 mm." "roughly has the head-body length of 140 mm (skull about 40 mm; trunk about 100 mm)".
- Predator mass: "we consider the body mass to be 12-14 kg for R. giganticus and 4-6 kg for R. robustus." SI Table 4: mean of HBL and SKL regressions = 3.85 kg (V13605); SI Table 2: limb-diameter mean = 4.13 kg (V13605).
- Ontogeny of predator: "the epiphysis and shaft of long bones are fused in IVPP V14155 and in the R. robustus skeleton described below".

## Han2023: Han et al. 2023 Sci Rep, *Repenomamus* attacking *Psittacosaurus*

- Specimen: "The new fossil (Weihai Ziguang Shi Yan School Museum WZSSM] specimen VF000011)". Not gut content.
- Prey: "The dinosaur skeleton is complete. The skull preserves all three autapomorphies that diagnose P. lujiatunensis". "we estimate its body mass to have been 10.6 ± 6.0 kg at time of death. We estimate its age to have been at least 6.5 years, and more likely closer to 10 years".
- Predator: "The skeleton is nearly complete; only the distal end of the tail is missing." "Developmental mass extrapolation and long bone circumference-body mass scaling relationships provide a mass of 3.43 ± 1.42 kg for the mammal." "the long bone epiphyses of the mammal are fused ... Probably, this individual was a subadult".
- Interaction: "The mammal's left hand grips the lower jaw of the dinosaur ... the hindfoot gripping the dinosaur's left shin ... The mammal died while biting two of the dinosaur's left anterior dorsal ribs".
- Flag: Table 1 gives Psittacosaurus femur minimum shaft circumference = 17.7 mm against femur L 137.7 mm, while their own mass method (Methods p.7) uses a femur circumference of 69.362 mm for a large individual. 17.7 may be a diameter or a typo. Check against the Supplementary Information before using it.
- Han et al. describe Hu 2005's prey as "a much smaller, perinatal Psittacosaurus sp." That disagrees with Hu 2005's "not from an embryo" and worn teeth.
